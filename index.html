Step 2 · Mortgage Protection Appointment Script — Running the Appointment

Walk through this in order on every appointment — introduction, fact-finding, uncovering the "why," presenting options, and closing the application.

1. Introduction & how it works

"Just so you know how this call will go, I'm what they call a field underwriter and broker. I sit down with about a couple dozen families a week to figure out whether mortgage protection is a necessity or a luxury. Legally we can't over-insure you, and ethically we don't want to under-insure you. My job is to build a case for suitability so we can get you approved today."

I'll ask some basic questions about your health, finances, and mortgage. Once we go through that, it usually becomes obvious whether this is something you need. We represent over 30 A-rated companies — names you've probably heard, like Mutual of Omaha, Fidelity, and Foresters.

Why families choose mortgage protection: families already have buckets of assets — stocks, 401(k)s, insurance through work — and don't want that bucket to go to a mortgage company when they pass, or be affected by taxes. That's where mortgage protection comes in.

2. Verify mortgage & budget
  • Verify the mortgage amount, monthly payment, refinance vs. new purchase, and how long they plan to stay
  • Total household income vs. monthly mortgage payment (back-of-the-napkin estimate)
  • Write income per person and type of income on the page — writing it out together builds the "why"
  • Ask about car notes, insurance, subscriptions, day care, phone, utilities, food, fun money, debt
  • Any 401k, savings, or life insurance already being paid into? Name each asset and its purpose — do they want to use it to pay the mortgage? (No.)

"So at the end of each month you have left over ____ — is that right, or is my math off?" Get them to agree to an amount; this overcomes "let me think about it" later.

3. Verify health info

Confirm height, weight, medications, health history, nicotine/tobacco use. "Anything you tell me is just to help me best place you with coverage so we can get you approved today."

  • What medications has the doctor prescribed?
  • When was the last time you were hospitalized?
  • Any other health situations from the past 15 years?

Nothing is "serious," even if it is — keep them comfortable sharing. Be curious and assumptive.

4. Close the gap — assumption questions

Under 50 (full/partial payoff): "I'm assuming you want to make sure your spouse and kids don't have to lose the home or move in with family if you passed away or couldn't make payments — or am I misunderstanding?"

60+, spouse is beneficiary: "I'm assuming you want to make sure we don't lose the home and equity or go into foreclosure — or am I misunderstanding?"

60+, kids are beneficiary: "I'm assuming you want to make sure your kids don't get burdened by the mortgage and end up losing the home."

50–60 year olds: go by health. Healthy → full/partial payoff. Unhealthy → critical period / equity protection.

Digging in: "If tomorrow your best friend ___ didn't come home, what's the current game plan?" Who's affected, how long could they survive, would they sell, could they carry the mortgage on one income? For kids-as-beneficiary: would they take on the mortgage plus their own expenses, how long do homes nearby take to sell, would they want to make that decision while grieving?

5. Transition to options

Ages 18–40: usually first home, biggest asset, still building savings — often protect the full payoff. Term or IUL for the mortgage duration.

Ages 40–60: more equity and possibly two incomes — often protect part of it, giving the family options to reduce and stay, or sell with more equity.

Ages 60+: usually fixed income — critical period / equity protection often makes more sense than a full payoff, giving breathing room to decide.

"Which of those three sounds most like your situation — and why did that one stick out?" Then: have them grab a pen, options are coming.

6. Present options

Full payoff: Option One — full payoff for X years at ___/mo. Option Two — partial ("his and hers" for couples), roughly half the mortgage. Option Three — half the mortgage combined with an accidental plan that pays off the full mortgage if death is accidental.

Partial payoff: Option One — 75% of balance. Option Two — 50% of balance. Option Three — same combined term + accidental structure.

Critical / equity protection: level premiums shown as 12, 9, or 6 months of coverage (months × monthly mortgage payment = plan amount). Option One — most months of coverage. Option Two — shorter period, lower monthly. Option Three — a third monthly amount.

"Which one are you most gravitating toward?" Then be quiet. Silence is where the sale is made.

7. Close & begin the application

"Let's begin that application and get you approved. The carrier we're applying with is ___. You'll get a policy packet in the mail we'll review together in two weeks."

Begin the application, verify everything, and enter the Social Security number last: "This carrier isn't going to come do blood work — they determine your health using your Social Security number, connected to your medical record. It also confirms the right person passed so they don't pay the wrong beneficiary. Go ahead with that 9-digit social."

Aged Lead Script — full call, start to finish
1. Mindset

Own the conversation, not the count.

Every call is a diagnosis, not a pitch. You're not chasing appointments — you're finding out who actually needs this, and disqualifying who doesn't. Confidence and tonality close more than raw dials ever will. Track your activity because it sharpens you, not because more equals better.

2. Opening

Tone: very relaxed, slow, slightly confused — like you're the one piecing it together, not selling anything.

"Hey (name)... it's Rhina..." (voice trails, low-key)
"I know my call is probably coming completely out of the blue for you right now..." (pause 1-2 seconds, let that land)

"I was actually looking through our archives and ran across a quick inquiry you had filled out a while back... it looked like it was regarding some mortgage protection options for the home there on (street name/city)..." (pause)

"I know it's been a minute since you looked at that, but I was just curious... did you ever find a plan to protect the home if something unexpected were to happen to you — if you pass away or became disabled — or are you still having to walk that tightrope right now?"

"Out of the blue" lowers their guard immediately — most telemarketers pretend they aren't interrupting anything. "Looking through our archives... a while back" explains why you're calling now, so it doesn't feel like a mass-dialer list. "Walk that tightrope" softly names the risk without ever sounding aggressive.

Optional, only if you're recording calls for training/compliance: "By the way, this call will be recorded for training and quality purposes, okay?"

Handling the first response

"I don't remember doing that." → "Yeah, that's completely fair — it's been quite a while, so I didn't expect you to remember it right off the top of your head. It basically just handles making sure the mortgage gets paid off for the family if you or (spouse) passed away or became disabled. Just out of curiosity, is that something you guys ever got sorted out, or is it still a gap for you?"

"We already took care of it." → "Oh, fantastic, I'm glad you got that locked in to protect the family. Just so I can update your file here and stop our system from reaching back out... did you end up going with a full payoff plan, or did you just get something basic to cover the payments for a year or two?" (this opens the door to check for gaps or a rate-shopping opportunity)

"Yeah, I'm still looking / what are the prices?" → "Gotcha. Well, the first part of this is pretty basic. Before we even talk about numbers, it's really more about figuring out what you might already have in place — like corporate benefits or existing policies — and comparing that to what your mortgage balance looks like today. That way we can see if there's actually an exposure there. Is that something you'd want to glance at?"

The disarm

"My job's pretty easy here — just to make sure we get you the right information, 'cause you might not even need this."

"Look, if you need this — because not everyone does, like if you have a bunch of money in the bank, or you owe less than $10,000 on your mortgage, then you don't need it. But if you do need it, I have about 40 different carriers in your state, so I'll be able to look at every A-rated company to find the one that's going to give you the highest level of protection at the lowest price. That's the only thing I can't control — the insurance companies determine who gets approved, not me. But I do look at all of them to see who's gonna give us the best shot, okay?"

3. Home ownership & mortgage details

"How long have you owned the home for?"

"Is it just you that owns the home, or do you own it with anybody else?" (if spouse/co-owner: a light line like "she's the queen of the castle, huh?" works well here)

Prior coverage check

"Have you ever looked into coverage like this before, or have any idea how it works?"

Mortgage details

"So what are you paying each month for the mortgage?" (write it down)
"And how much do you have left on the home?"
"Is that a 15, 20, or 30-year loan — and how many years do you have left on it?"
"And (name), how old are you?"

4. The burden question

A bit of an awkward question, but ask it anyway:

"If, heaven forbid, something happened to you yesterday — I'd say yesterday because I don't want to speak anything into existence — who would be responsible for picking up the pieces and making sure the mortgage payments and all the bills were still taken care of?" (let them answer — spouse, daughter, whoever)

"And what's her/his name?"

"Now if you had to imagine (name) in that situation — what's one thing, or I guess one burden, that you really want to make sure she's/he's not gonna have to deal with?" (most answer "the mortgage" or "the house")

"And out of all the bills, why the house?"

If kids are involved

"I love that you're looking into this already for your kids — that's great."
"Do any of the kids still live in the house with you?"
"And (name), what kind of has you looking into this now? Has anything changed with your income, or have you seen somebody put their kids or loved ones through something like this?"

5. Existing coverage — "what do you have?"

"Does she/he already have something in place like this to protect it?"

"Is she/he working right now?"

"So God forbid something were to happen to you, would she/he be trying to stay in the house, or trying not to lose it?" (let them answer — often reveals the real emotional reason, like grandkids nearby or school district — acknowledge it warmly)

Their own coverage

"And most of my clients have life insurance — what do you have?" (if none: "You don't have any right now? Okay, got it." if yes, keep going:)

"How much do you have?"
"When did you guys get that coverage?"
"And what kind of has you looking into this now? Has anything changed with your health, or have you seen somebody put their spouse through something like this?"
"And by the way — was any of that coverage for the house, or was that more for final expenses, just making sure (spouse) is okay for a little bit?"

This distinction matters — most existing coverage is for final expenses, not the mortgage specifically, which is exactly the gap you're there to fill.

6. The pain summary — "so the goal seems to be..."

Say it slowly — it hands everything they just told you back to them as their own conclusion.

"So the goal seems to be able to make sure that nothing ever happens to (name), and she/he can stay in the home forever, or at least pocket all the equity and not be forced to do anything. Right?" (let them agree. Add when kids are involved: "and the goal is to keep the home in the family, right?")

7. The close

Once you handle an objection above and get a micro-agreement (e.g. "No, we don't have a plan yet," or "I guess they'd have to sell the house"), transition straight to booking with this low-stakes, status-frame script.

"Gotcha. Well, look, (name), because this inquiry is a bit older, we wouldn't want you to dive into anything anyway unless it actually made complete sense for your budget."

"What most people do is we just set up a quick 10 to 15-minute window over Zoom or a phone call. We can look at what you might already qualify for based on your health, see if there are any glaring exposures with the mortgage, and let you look at the options. If it fits, great. If not, at least you know where you stand."

"I have a brief window open on (day 1) at (time), or does (day 2) around (time) tend to fit your schedule better?"

8. The trust moment — sending your license

Send this before you move into qualifying — not after you hang up. Say it out loud while you send it, so you hear them receive it.

"(Name), before I go any further, I'm going to send you over my license and my digital card right now, just so you have that."

"Go ahead and take a look at your text message real quick — I just want you to see that, and then I'll let you know what I need from you." (wait for them to open it)

"So the first thing there — that digital card. First thing there, it's important to me: it's my family."

"The second thing is my National Producer Number. So when you go on the Florida registry to check my license — which I encourage you to do — you'll see my mailing address there too. And that's why I send you my driver's license as well, just so you know you're dealing with a real person."

"So my name again is Rhina, and the company is Symmetry Financial Group. My Florida state license number is G355268 — that's Life, including Variable Annuities, and Health. You can look me up on the Florida Department of Financial Services website if you'd like to verify." (If they ask for your NPN — that's 3993466.)

This takes an extra thirty seconds and does more trust-building than anything else in the call — people aren't skeptical of the product, they're skeptical of the person on the phone. This kills that before you ask a single health question.

9. Qualifying — primary applicant

"Okay — I have a solid understanding of what we're trying to do here. What I'm going to do now is ask you some health questions real quick, just to make sure we're looking at the right stuff. None of these are knockout questions — the more I know, the better, so I can choose a carrier that's gonna give us the best chances. Okay?"

"Some of these are going to sound a little random, but I have to ask them anyway — and if any of your answers are 'yes,' don't worry about it, I just need your full honesty so I can match you with the right company."

  • "What's your date of birth?"
  • "What's your height and weight?"
  • "Are you a smoker or non-smoker?"
  • "Any prescription medications you're currently taking?"
  • "Any prescription medications you've been prescribed but may no longer be taking?" (dive deep into this one — don't let it slide by)
  • "Anything major health-wise — cancer, heart attack, stroke at all?"
  • "And anything less major — high blood pressure, diabetes, high cholesterol?"

Splitting major vs. less major like this reads much more naturally out loud than listing everything flat. For any "yes," follow up right then:

"How long ago were you diagnosed?" · "What are you taking for it?" · "Has it been working for you?"

That last one matters — a controlled, well-managed condition reads very differently to underwriting than an unmanaged one, and it's also just a normal, caring thing to ask.

  • "This form says you owe about (loan amount) on your loan — is that still correct?"
  • "Any surgeries or hospitalizations in the past 10 years?"
  • "And they do look at things like reckless driving, DUIs, felonies — would anything like that pop up for you?"
10. Qualifying — spouse / co-borrower + work & schedule

If there's a spouse or co-borrower on the loan, go through the same set for them:

  • "Can I get her/his full name and birthday?"
  • "What's her/his height and weight?"
  • "Is she/he a smoker or non-smoker?"
  • "Is she/he currently taking any medications?"
  • "Any medical conditions or concerns for her/him — same as we just went through?"

Work & schedule

"What do you do for work, and what's your schedule like?"
"And what does (spouse) do, and what's their schedule?"

If (spouse) doesn't work / stays home: "So it might be challenging for (spouse) to keep up with the mortgage if something happened to you?" → "Well, that's exactly what this protection does — it makes sure (spouse) could still keep up with those payments, even if something happened to you."

11. Budget

"Okay, so what I am going to do in the meantime is run this info through our needs analysis software and see who's going to give us the best shot. While it's doing its thing, I am going to ask you some questions about your budget. This has to make sense — it would be really silly for me to show you something that we couldn't afford every month, right?"

If they mention an older policy: "Okay, while I'm putting this into the system here, they're going to have me put information about your older policy. So do me a favor — I need the name of that policy, and the amount that came out last month. There is such a thing as being over-insured, so we have to make sure we're in compliance here."

12. Transition into research

"Okay, I think I have everything I need. I work with more than 28 different carriers, so what I'm going to do between now and our next call is dig through those and make sure you're matched with the right one for your situation."

13. Book the appointment

Remind them the spouse/co-borrower needs to be present for this next call.

"Since (spouse) is also part of this, I want to make sure they're able to join us on that next call too — does that work?"

Position yourself as busy (your appointments are always the NEXT day — you need time to research carriers first; don't offer "today")

"I've got quite a few of these requests coming in, so my schedule's been pretty full. Let's plan for tomorrow — would morning or afternoon work better for you both?"

"I have an opening at (time 1) or I can squeeze you in at (time 2). Which of those works better for you and (spouse)?"

If neither time works: "Okay, no problem — what does your schedule usually look like, mornings or afternoons? And do either of you have things like appointments or client hours I should work around?" Once you know their general pattern, offer your next available day with two more specific times.

Tie-down the appointment

"And you're positive that time works for both of you together, correct?"

"Okay, grab a pen and paper and let me know when you're ready — go ahead and write down 'mortgage protection.' And what time did we say again?"

"And if for any reason you can't make it, please let me know as soon as you can — I don't want to miss out on helping another family who could use that time."

Zoom and email

"Are you familiar with Zoom? … Great — what's a good email so I can send you the appointment invite with the Zoom link as soon as we hang up?" (when they spell out an email, confirm ambiguous letters phonetically — "Z as in zebra?" "B as in boy?")

"As soon as we get off the phone, I'm going to email you that invitation. Open it and click the Zoom link as soon as it arrives — it'll walk you through installing the app if you don't already have it."

"You'll also get a text message from me to confirm the appointment, kind of like how a doctor's office does it."

If they don't have internet, a computer, or a cell phone (landline only): "No problem at all — we can just do everything over the phone instead. I'll walk you through your options verbally, and if you'd like anything in writing afterward, I can mail it to you directly." Confirm the callback number and a good time to catch them; if they want anything mailed, get their mailing address now.

Grace period and sign-off

"Okay, I have us down for (day) at (time). Sometimes these appointments run a little long, so if you could give me some grace on either side — maybe 15 minutes — I'd really appreciate it. And if I'm running behind, I'll be sure to let you know."

If married/co-borrower — recap by name before the why tie-back: "So the loan can be protected if, God forbid, something happens to either you or (spouse), okay?"

Tie back to their why before you hang up

"Before I let you go — you mentioned earlier that (their reason from the burden question) is what got you looking into this. I want you to know that's exactly what we're going to make sure is taken care of when we talk on (day)."

"Have a great day, and we'll talk to you on (day) at (time)!"

Do NOT say "thank you" here — it reads as if you did them a favor, not the other way around.

When you follow up after research

"(Name), I went through your information and checked it against the carriers I work with — here's what you actually qualify for…" (present your findings and the real options/pricing — they accept or decline from there)

14. Objection handling — NEPQ playbook

For older leads specifically. Calm, slightly curious, downplayed tone — never debate, never use a standard rebuttal. Validate, lean back, and ask a neutral question that gets them to name their own gap.

1. "I'm not interested"

"That's completely fair. I mean, we haven't even really looked at your situation to see if you'd even qualify or if this would even be relevant for you yet... so it makes total sense that you aren't interested. Just so I don't get in your way... are you not interested because you already have an active plan that completely pays off the mortgage balance if you pass away, or did you just assume I was a pushy salesperson?"

2. "I don't remember filling that out"

"Oh yeah, I wouldn't expect you to remember it right off the top of your head — it's been quite a while since you looked at it. It basically just handles making sure your home on (street name) gets completely taken care of for your family if you pass away or have a critical illness. Out of curiosity... since it's been a minute, did you guys ever find a separate way to protect that equity, or is that still an exposed area if something happened?"

3. "I don't want this"

"No problem at all, (name). We definitely don't want to push anything on you that you don't want. Just so I can properly note your file and stop our system from bothering you... is it that you don't want a policy because your family already has the liquid cash to pay the house off tomorrow if you died, or is it just not a priority right now?"

4. "I'm already taken care of / I have a policy"

"Oh, awesome! I'm glad you got that locked in to protect the family. Just so I can update your file accurately... did you end up putting a specific plan in place that completely wipes out the remaining mortgage principal tax-free, or is it more of a traditional regular life insurance policy through work?"

5. "My spouse and I have a lot of money / we're wealthy"

"Gotcha, so you guys are in a really solid financial position, which is great. I guess... from an asset perspective... if you passed away unexpectedly, would your family prefer to pull (mortgage balance, e.g. $400,000) out of your hard-earned investments and personal cash reserves to pay off the bank, or would it make more sense to let an insurance company cut that check so your personal wealth stays completely intact for your heirs?"

6. "I have a trust"

"Oh, excellent, having a trust set up is smart for avoiding probate. Just so I'm clear on how you structured it... does the trust itself hold enough independent cash to instantly pay off the entire mortgage balance to the bank, or would the estate still be forced to liquidate other properties or investments to keep the home from being sold?"

7. "My kids will take care of it / take over the house"

"It's great that you have supportive kids. I guess... if you were to pass away tomorrow... would they realistically be able to easily absorb your entire monthly mortgage payment on top of their own bills, or would that force them into a position where they'd have to sell the home just to get out from underneath your debt?"

8. "It's too expensive"

"Yeah, everything is getting so crazy expensive right now, so I completely get it. But we actually haven't even looked at your health or the options to see what the numbers would be yet. Just out of curiosity... what is your game plan to protect the family from losing the home if the worst happened, if looking at a custom, budget-friendly option isn't on the table?"

9. "I've received way too many calls about this"

"Oh, look... I completely apologize. You have probably been absolutely hounded by pushy sales people over the last few weeks, haven't you?" (let them vent, then continue) "Honestly, that's exactly why I'm calling. Because the lead is older, I wanted to personally see if you actually got what you needed so I can completely pull your phone number out of our regional system and stop the madness for you. Did you actually find a plan that protects the equity, or are they all just calling without actually helping you out?"

15. Additional objections

These aren't covered in the NEPQ playbook above — keep them handy for anything else that comes up.

"Take me off"

"No problem, I can do that for you. Before I do — can I ask, is it that the timing's off right now, or you've already got this handled somewhere else?" (if they still say no or insist, honor it immediately, set the lead aside, don't call again)

"My spouse is already taken care of"

"Oh, so does she have life insurance already?" (if no: "Okay, so just so I understand — if something happened to you, how long would she be able to go before she'd need to dip into savings, or maybe pick up extra work, just to stay secure?" or, softer: "Why would she have to touch her savings when she could have this instead?")

On lender-offered coverage / home insurance

"The lender can't actually offer mortgage protection the way I can — home insurance protects the lender, not your family. So really, what happens to your family and the mortgage if something happens to you or your income?"

On income/disability

"Disability income covers a portion of your income if you're ever unable to work — will you and your family be fine without that?"

If they push back further

  • Don't argue. Ever. Use: "I hear you."
  • If they say things are tight — don't push, just acknowledge: "What I'm hearing is things are tight right now." Then offer a solution sized to that.
  • If they mention a child — that's a dependent. Bring a solution built around protecting that child.
  • Never close the call without walking through their options and what's actually necessary for their situation.

If they mention a notice/letter

"Go ahead and just put that notice to the side — we'll take care of it from here."

If they ask "What's an IMO?"

"I work through an IMO — that's basically an agency that partners with a lot of different insurance companies at once. Instead of being tied to just one company, that partnership is what lets me shop your case across all of them and find the one that actually fits you best."

"I already did this / already talked to an agent"

"That makes sense — but every agent is different. Some agents only work with one or two companies, so maybe that agent didn't go over every option, or didn't have as many carriers to compare against. I work with a lot of different carriers, so let me take a look and see what's out there for you specifically — there's no harm in comparing."

"Can you just email me the options?"

"That's a great idea, and I'll be super brief. Let me just verify your information real quick, then I'll get your options together and we'll go over them together."

"I already have living benefits"

"That's actually a separate feature — living benefits can be added onto a policy, and they let you access money while you're alive if you get diagnosed with something serious. What we're talking about here is different — mortgage protection is specifically there to cover the mortgage itself if something happens to you. The two can actually work together, they're not the same thing."

Recorre esto en orden en cada cita — introducción, descubrimiento de información, descubrir el "porqué", presentar opciones, y cerrar la solicitud.

1. Introducción y cómo funciona

"Solo para que sepas cómo va a ir esta llamada, soy lo que llaman una suscriptora e intermediaria de campo. Me reúno con un par de docenas de familias por semana para determinar si la protección hipotecaria es una necesidad o un lujo. Legalmente no podemos sobre-asegurarte, y éticamente no queremos sub-asegurarte. Mi trabajo es construir un caso de idoneidad para que podamos aprobarte hoy."

"Te haré algunas preguntas básicas sobre tu salud, finanzas e hipoteca. Una vez que revisemos eso, normalmente se vuelve obvio si esto es algo que necesitas. Representamos a más de 30 compañías calificadas A — nombres que probablemente conoces, como Mutual of Omaha, Fidelity y Foresters."

Por qué las familias eligen la protección hipotecaria: las familias ya tienen bolsas de activos — acciones, 401(k)s, seguro a través del trabajo — y no quieren que esa bolsa se vaya a una compañía hipotecaria cuando fallezcan, o que se vea afectada por impuestos. Ahí es donde entra la protección hipotecaria.

2. Verificar hipoteca y presupuesto
  • Verifica el monto de la hipoteca, el pago mensual, si fue refinanciamiento o compra nueva, y cuánto tiempo planean quedarse
  • Ingreso total del hogar vs. pago mensual de la hipoteca (cálculo aproximado)
  • Anota el ingreso por persona y tipo de ingreso en la hoja — escribirlo juntos ayuda a construir el "porqué"
  • Pregunta sobre pagos de auto, seguro, suscripciones, guardería, teléfono, servicios, comida, dinero de gustos, deudas
  • ¿Tienen 401k, ahorros o seguro de vida ya vigente? Nombra cada activo y su propósito — ¿quieren usarlo para pagar la hipoteca? (No.)

"Entonces al final de cada mes les queda ____ — ¿es correcto, o mis números están un poco equivocados?" Logra que acuerden un monto; esto ayuda a superar el "necesito pensarlo" más adelante.

3. Verificar información de salud

Confirma estatura, peso, medicamentos, historial de salud, uso de nicotina/tabaco. "Todo lo que me digas es solo para ayudarte a colocarte con la cobertura correcta, para que podamos aprobarte hoy."

  • ¿Qué medicamentos te ha recetado el doctor?
  • ¿Cuándo fue la última vez que estuviste hospitalizado(a)?
  • ¿Alguna otra situación de salud en los últimos 15 años?

Nada es "grave," aunque lo sea — que se sientan cómodos compartiendo. Sé curiosa y asertiva.

4. Cerrar la brecha — preguntas de suposición

Menores de 50 (pago total/parcial): "Supongo que la razón por la que estamos viendo esto es porque quieres asegurarte de que tu cónyuge e hijos no tengan que perder la casa o mudarse con familiares si tú fallecieras o te enfermaras y no pudieras hacer los pagos — ¿o estoy entendiendo mal?"

60+, cónyuge es beneficiario: "Supongo que quieres asegurarte de que no perdamos la casa ni el patrimonio, o que no entremos en ejecución hipotecaria — ¿o estoy entendiendo mal?"

60+, hijos son beneficiarios: "Supongo que quieres asegurarte de que tus hijos no carguen con la hipoteca y terminen perdiendo la casa."

Entre 50 y 60 años: depende de la salud. Saludable → pago total/parcial. No saludable → protección del período crítico/patrimonio.

Profundizando: "Si mañana tu mejor amigo(a) ___ no llegara a casa, ¿cuál es el plan actual?" ¿Quién se vería afectado, cuánto tiempo podrían sobrevivir, tendrían que vender, podrían mantener la hipoteca con un solo ingreso? Para beneficiarios hijos: ¿asumirían la hipoteca además de sus propios gastos, cuánto tiempo tardan en venderse las casas cerca, querrían tomar esa decisión mientras están de luto?

5. Transición a las opciones

Edades 18–40: usualmente es su primera casa, su mayor activo, aún construyendo ahorros — suelen proteger el pago total. Término o IUL por la duración de la hipoteca.

Edades 40–60: más patrimonio y posiblemente dos ingresos — suelen proteger una parte, dando a la familia opciones de reducir y quedarse, o vender con más patrimonio.

Edades 60+: usualmente ingreso fijo — la protección del período crítico/patrimonio suele tener más sentido que un pago total, dando margen para decidir.
