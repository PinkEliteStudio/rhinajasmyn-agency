Step 2 · Mortgage Protection Appointment Script — Running the Appointment

Walk through this in order on every appointment — introduction, fact-finding, uncovering the "why," presenting options, and closing the application.

1. Introduction & how it works

"Just so you know how this call will go, I'm what they call a field underwriter and broker. I sit down with about a couple dozen families a week to figure out whether mortgage protection is a necessity or a luxury. Legally we can't over-insure you, and ethically we don't want to under-insure you. My job is to build a case for suitability so we can get you approved today."

I'll ask some basic questions about your health, finances, and mortgage. Once we go through that, it usually becomes obvious whether this is something you need. We represent over 30 A-rated companies — names you've probably heard, like Mutual of Omaha, Fidelity, and Foresters.

Why families choose mortgage protection: families already have buckets of assets — stocks, 401(k)s, insurance through work — and don't want that bucket to go to a mortgage company when they pass, or be affected by taxes. That's where mortgage protection comes in.

2. Verify mortgage & budget
  • Verify the mortgage amount, monthly payment, refinance vs. new purchase, and how long they plan to stay
  • Total household income vs. monthly mortgage payment (back-of-the-napkin estimate)
  • Write income per person and type of income on the page — writing it out together builds the "why"
  • Ask about car notes, insurance, subscriptions, day care, phone, utilities, food, fun money, debt
  • Any 401k, savings, or life insurance already being paid into? Name each asset and its purpose — do they want to use it to pay the mortgage? (No.)

"So at the end of each month you have left over ____ — is that right, or is my math off?" Get them to agree to an amount; this overcomes "let me think about it" later.

3. Verify health info

Confirm height, weight, medications, health history, nicotine/tobacco use. "Anything you tell me is just to help me best place you with coverage so we can get you approved today."

  • What medications has the doctor prescribed?
  • When was the last time you were hospitalized?
  • Any other health situations from the past 15 years?

Nothing is "serious," even if it is — keep them comfortable sharing. Be curious and assumptive.

4. Close the gap — assumption questions

Under 50 (full/partial payoff): "I'm assuming you want to make sure your spouse and kids don't have to lose the home or move in with family if you passed away or couldn't make payments — or am I misunderstanding?"

60+, spouse is beneficiary: "I'm assuming you want to make sure we don't lose the home and equity or go into foreclosure — or am I misunderstanding?"

60+, kids are beneficiary: "I'm assuming you want to make sure your kids don't get burdened by the mortgage and end up losing the home."

50–60 year olds: go by health. Healthy → full/partial payoff. Unhealthy → critical period / equity protection.

Digging in: "If tomorrow your best friend ___ didn't come home, what's the current game plan?" Who's affected, how long could they survive, would they sell, could they carry the mortgage on one income? For kids-as-beneficiary: would they take on the mortgage plus their own expenses, how long do homes nearby take to sell, would they want to make that decision while grieving?

5. Transition to options

Ages 18–40: usually first home, biggest asset, still building savings — often protect the full payoff. Term or IUL for the mortgage duration.

Ages 40–60: more equity and possibly two incomes — often protect part of it, giving the family options to reduce and stay, or sell with more equity.

Ages 60+: usually fixed income — critical period / equity protection often makes more sense than a full payoff, giving breathing room to decide.

"Which of those three sounds most like your situation — and why did that one stick out?" Then: have them grab a pen, options are coming.

6. Present options

Full payoff: Option One — full payoff for X years at ___/mo. Option Two — partial ("his and hers" for couples), roughly half the mortgage. Option Three — half the mortgage combined with an accidental plan that pays off the full mortgage if death is accidental.

Partial payoff: Option One — 75% of balance. Option Two — 50% of balance. Option Three — same combined term + accidental structure.

Critical / equity protection: level premiums shown as 12, 9, or 6 months of coverage (months × monthly mortgage payment = plan amount). Option One — most months of coverage. Option Two — shorter period, lower monthly. Option Three — a third monthly amount.

"Which one are you most gravitating toward?" Then be quiet. Silence is where the sale is made.

7. Close & begin the application

"Let's begin that application and get you approved. The carrier we're applying with is ___. You'll get a policy packet in the mail we'll review together in two weeks."

Begin the application, verify everything, and enter the Social Security number last: "This carrier isn't going to come do blood work — they determine your health using your Social Security number, connected to your medical record. It also confirms the right person passed so they don't pay the wrong beneficiary. Go ahead with that 9-digit social."

Aged Lead Script — full call, start to finish
1. How to sound — read this first

Say WHEN, never IF. "When something happens to you..." There is no "if" with death. "If" lets their brain say "not me" and relax.

They have heard every agent say the same thing. What makes you different is how you sound and what you ask next. About 70% of the call is your tone and 30% is your words. Your tone is how they read your intention.

  • curious Light and interested. Use it to find information: "Walk me through..."
  • confused "Hold on... help me understand." Use it when they are vague. Their brain thinks you didn't get it, so they explain more.
  • concerned Slower, lower voice, hand on your chest even on the phone. Use it on pain and consequence questions.
  • challenge Calm but firm. Only after you have built the pain. Never in the first few minutes.
  • playful A little smile in your voice. It breaks their pattern and drops their guard.

The dots "..." mean stop and breathe. Fast questions get surface answers. Slow questions get real answers.

Never say: "How are you today?" · "Is this a good time?" · "Do you have two minutes?" They have heard those from every salesperson and their guard goes up. Don't sound excited, don't sound like you're reading, and don't sound timid. Be the calm expert in the middle.

  • Aged and VA/refi leads: keep your voice flat and helpful, like someone checking a file, not hunting a sale. The word "exposed" is your friend — it names the risk with no pressure.
  • Your facial expression controls your tone. Smile on playful lines, look concerned on pain lines.
2. The opening — get their attention

Tone: calm, slow, a little confused. Your first 7 to 12 seconds decide if they get curious or put up a wall.

familiar "Hi, is this (name)? ... (Name), hey, it's Rhina... Rhina Delgado, here in Florida."

"I just had a sec to get back to you here — I got your request about the coverage that pays off the house in case anything happens to you." (pause, let it land)

Optional playful line (pick whichever fits your mood and the person — both work, neither is required)

playful "I'll be honest — this is probably not how you wanted your (day) interrupted... but I promise I'll keep it quick."

playful "Would it ruin your (day) if I told you this is one of those calls you forgot you asked for?"

"Now (name), how long have you owned the home?"

"Is it just you that owns the home, or do you own it with somebody else?" (if spouse: "so she's the queen of the castle, huh?" works well here)

"And have you ever looked into coverage like this before — any idea how it works?"

The burden question concerned

"If, heaven forbid, something happened to you — who would be responsible for picking up the pieces and keeping up with the mortgage and the bills?" (let them answer) "And what's her/his name?"

"If you picture (name) in that spot... what's one burden you really don't want her/him to deal with?" (most say "the house") "Out of all the bills... why the house?"

If kids are involved: "Do any of the kids still live in the house with you?" · "What has you looking into this now? Did something change... or did you see someone go through this?"

The disarm

"Well, my job's pretty easy here, (name) — it's just to make sure we get you the right information, 'cause you might not even need this."

"If you need this... because not everybody does. If you have a bunch of money in the bank, or you owe less than $10,000 on your mortgage, you don't need it. But if you do need it, I have about 40 different carriers in the state of (state). So I'll be able to look at every A-rated company to find the one that's going to give you the highest level of protection, at the lowest price. That's the only thing I can't control — the insurance companies decide who gets approved, not me — but I look at all of them to see who gives us the best shot, okay?"

Status frame — you lead the call

"The first part of this call is pretty basic. It's just for me to see what you have in place now... when something happens to you... compared to what you'd want your family to have. Just to see if there's a gap. ... Then at the end, if it looks like it might be what you're looking for... we can talk about possible next steps. Would that help?"

curious "And just so I understand... what were you hoping could happen... by possibly getting this in place?"

Make it real — consequence & future pain

concerned "So if that happened... how many months could (name) keep paying the mortgage, the car, and the bills... without your income?"

challenge "And if nothing changes... and another year goes by... what happens to her and the kids?"

If they downplay it: concerned "Are you 100% sure... she could keep the house?"

The pain summary — "so the goal seems to be..."

Say it slowly. You're handing their own words back to them as their own conclusion.

"So the goal seems to be making sure that if something happened to you, (name) can stay in the home... or at least keep all the equity and not be forced to sell. Right?" (if kids: "and keep the home in the family, right?")

Optional, only if you record calls for training/compliance: "By the way, this call will be recorded for training and quality purposes, okay?"

3. If they push back at hello

Calm, curious, downplayed. Never argue, never use a canned rebuttal. Agree with them, lean back, and ask one neutral question that makes them name their own gap.

"How are you?" (they ask you first)
playful "Oh, just trying to stay out of trouble... how about you?"

"I'm busy, call me later"
"Totally fine. ... Before I let you go... can I, um... can I ask you one thing? ... When something happens to you... who makes the house payment?" (most people stay on after this)

"I'm not interested"
"That's completely fair. We haven't even looked at your situation to see if this would even make sense for you yet... Just so I don't get in your way... is it because you already have a plan that pays off the whole house when something happens to you, or did you just figure I was another pushy salesperson?"

"I don't remember filling that out"
"Oh yeah, I wouldn't expect you to remember — it's been a while. It's the plan that makes sure your home on (street) is taken care of for your family when something happens to you. Out of curiosity... did you ever find another way to protect that equity, or is it still exposed?"

"I don't want this"
"No problem at all, (name). We don't want to push anything on you. Just so I can note your file... is it that your family already has the cash to pay off the house the day something happens to you, or is it just not a priority right now?"

"I'm already covered / I have a policy"
"Oh, awesome, I'm glad you got that in place. Just so I update your file right... is it a plan that wipes out the whole mortgage tax-free, or more of a regular life policy through work?"

"We have a lot of money"
"Got it, so you're in a solid spot, which is great. I guess... when something happens to you... would your family rather pull (balance) out of your investments and savings to pay the bank, or let an insurance company write that check so your wealth stays whole for your family?"

"I have a trust"
"Oh, that's smart for avoiding probate. Just so I understand how you set it up... does the trust hold enough cash to pay off the whole mortgage right away, or would the estate have to sell something to keep the house?"

"My kids will take over the house"
"It's great you have kids who'd step up. I guess... when something happens to you... could they really take on your whole mortgage payment on top of their own bills, or would they end up having to sell just to get out from under it?"

"I've gotten way too many calls about this"
"Oh... I'm sorry. You've probably been hounded by pushy salespeople, haven't you?" (let them vent) "Honestly, that's why I'm calling. Your request is older, so I wanted to see if you actually got what you needed, so I can take your number out of the system and stop the calls. Did you find a plan that protects the equity... or are they all just calling without actually helping?"

"Take me off"
"No problem, I can do that. Before I do — is it that the timing's off, or you already have this handled somewhere else?" (if they insist, honor it right away and don't call again)

"I'm good, I'm good, no thank you" (fast brush-off)
"No worries at all — before you go, one quick thing: is that because you already have it covered, or just bad timing right now?" (if they answer, go to the matching line above; if they hang up, let them go)

4. Home & mortgage details

"What are you paying each month for the mortgage?" (write it down)
"And how much do you have left on the home?"
"Is that a 15, 20, or 30-year loan — and how many years are left?"
"And (name), how old are you?"

5. Build the gap — what's in place now

curious "Walk me through... what do you have in place right now... for when something happens to you?"

If it's through work: confused "Hold on... that's through your job, right? ... What happens to it when you leave, retire, or get let go?" Then: "And what's causing you to feel like that might not be enough?" (now they tell you why they need more — and they tell themselves)

If "My wife has money, she'll be fine": curious "Okay... what does she have? Savings, a 401k, her own life insurance?" Then concerned "And would you want her using her retirement... to pay off the house?"

If "We have enough equity": concerned "The only way to use that equity... is to sell the house. That takes months. ... Where do she and the kids live while it sells?"

PMI (use on almost everyone): curious "Do you know what your PMI actually pays out on?" ... concerned "It pays the bank. ... Not your family."

Home insurance / lender coverage: "Home insurance protects the bank, not your family. So what happens to your family and the mortgage when something happens to you?"

"I already have living benefits": "That's a separate feature — it lets you use money while you're alive if you get very sick. This plan is there to pay off the house. The two work together; they're not the same thing."

Their own coverage: "And most of my clients have some life insurance — what do you have? How much? When did you get it?" Then: "Was any of that for the house... or more for final expenses?" (most coverage is for final expenses, not the house — that's the gap)

"Is (name) working right now? ... When something happens to you, would she/he be trying to stay in the house... or trying not to lose it?"

6. Go deeper — precision probing

Most agents stop at the first problem and start fixing it. Don't take the bait. The deeper they go, the more they want to change. Save the feeling questions until you've asked a few others — trust grows as the call goes on.

Repeat their emotion word. They say "yeah, it worries me." You say only: concerned "Worries you..." Then stop. They will explain.

  • confused "How long has that been on your mind?"
  • concerned "Has that... had an impact on you?"
  • concerned "What's that doing to you?"
  • confused "In what way though?" (when the answer is vague)
  • curious "Can you give me an example... of when you thought about it?"
  • curious "What else do you feel might be going on?" (find more than one problem — one problem is a small gap)

If they mention someone who died or got sick, never skip it. concerned "Oh no... what happened? ... Did he have anything in place for his family?" Let them relive it. Either the coverage was a blessing, or not having it was a burden. Both build the why.

7. The trust moment — sending your license

Send this before you move into qualifying — not after you hang up. Say it out loud while you send it, so you hear them receive it.

"(Name), before I go any further, I'm going to send you over my license and my digital card right now, just so you have that."

"Go ahead and take a look at your text message real quick — I just want you to see that, and then I'll let you know what I need from you." (wait for them to open it)

"So the first thing there — that digital card. First thing there, it's important to me: it's my family."

"The second thing is my National Producer Number. So when you go on the Florida registry to check my license — which I encourage you to do — you'll see my mailing address there too. And that's why I send you my driver's license as well, just so you know you're dealing with a real person."

"So my name again is Rhina, and the company is Symmetry Financial Group. My Florida state license number is G355268 — that's Life, including Variable Annuities, and Health. You can look me up on the Florida Department of Financial Services website if you'd like to verify." (If they ask for your NPN — that's 3993466.)

This takes an extra thirty seconds and does more trust-building than anything else in the call — people aren't skeptical of the product, they're skeptical of the person on the phone. This kills that before you ask a single health question.

8. Bridge to health questions — pain, then solution

concerned "(Name), from everything you told me... when something happens to you, (spouse) has maybe (X) months before she has to sell the house or get a second job. ... That's not what you want for her."

"The only way to know if we can fix that... is a few quick health questions. ... Some people qualify for more than they think... some can't get it at all. ... Would it be okay if we see where you land?"

"None of these are knockout questions — the more I know, the better I can pick a carrier that gives us the best shot. Some will sound a little random, but I have to ask them. If any answer is 'yes,' don't worry, I just need your full honesty."

Ask these in a curious, neutral tone — not like you're reading a form. After a tough answer, just say "okay... thank you for telling me."

  • "What's your date of birth?"
  • "What's your height and weight?"
  • "Are you a smoker or non-smoker?"
  • "Any prescription medications you're taking now?"
  • "Any medications you've been prescribed but may no longer be taking?" (dig into this one)
  • "Anything major health-wise — cancer, heart attack, stroke at all?"
  • "And anything less major — high blood pressure, diabetes, high cholesterol?"

For any "yes": "How long ago were you diagnosed?" · "What are you taking for it?" · "Has it been working for you?" (a controlled condition reads very differently to underwriting)

  • "The form says you owe about (loan amount) — is that still right?"
  • "Any surgeries or hospital stays in the past 10 years?"
  • "They also look at things like reckless driving, DUIs, felonies — would anything like that come up?"
9. Spouse / co-borrower + work & schedule

If there's a spouse or co-borrower on the loan, go through the same set for them:

  • "Can I get her/his full name and birthday?"
  • "What's her/his height and weight?"
  • "Is she/he a smoker or non-smoker?"
  • "Is she/he taking any medications?"
  • "Any medical conditions for her/him — same as we just went through?"

"What do you do for work, and what's your schedule like?" · "And what does (spouse) do, and what's their schedule?"

If (spouse) doesn't work: "So it might be hard for (spouse) to keep up with the mortgage when something happens to you?" → "That's exactly what this protection does — it makes sure (spouse) can keep up with those payments."

10. Budget

"Okay, I'm going to run this through our needs analysis software to see who gives us the best shot. While it's working, a few questions about your budget. This has to make sense — it would be silly for me to show you something you couldn't afford every month, right?"

If they mention an older policy: "The system is having me put in your older policy too. I need the name of it and the amount that came out last month. There is such a thing as being over-insured, so we have to stay in compliance."

11. Book the appointment

"Okay, I think I have everything I need. I work with more than 28 carriers, so between now and our next call I'm going to dig through them and match you with the right one."

"And because your request is a bit older, I wouldn't want you to jump into anything unless it made complete sense for your budget. So we just set up a short Zoom — about 30 minutes — with you and (spouse). We look at what you qualify for and your options. If it fits, great. If not, at least you know where you stand."

Both spouses on the call. "Since (spouse) is part of this, I want them on that next call too. This covers the house you both live in. Does that work?"

Position yourself as busy (appointments are always the NEXT day — you need time to research; never offer "today")

"My schedule's been pretty full with these. Let's plan for tomorrow — is morning or evening better for you both? ... I have (time 1), or I can squeeze you in at (time 2). Which works better for you and (spouse)?"

If neither works: "What does your schedule usually look like, mornings or afternoons?" Then offer your next day with two times.

Zoom and email: "Are you familiar with Zoom? ... Great — what's a good email so I can send the invite as soon as we hang up?" (confirm tricky letters: "Z as in zebra?") "Open it and click the Zoom link when it arrives — it'll help you install the app if you need it. You'll also get a text from me to confirm, like a doctor's office does."

No internet or cell phone? "No problem — we'll do everything by phone, and I can mail anything you want in writing." Confirm the number and get a mailing address if needed.

12. Lock the show-up — don't hang up after the time

This is where most agents lose the appointment. Booking is not the finish line. Showing up is.

1. Make them say why. curious "(Name), just so I understand... why is it important for you and (spouse) to be on that call?" (now they sell themselves)

2. Find the blocker. "Is there anything that might come up... that would stop you from being there?" Solve it now.

3. Tie it down. "And you're sure that time works for both of you together?"

4. Write it down. "Grab a pen for me. ... Write down 'mortgage protection,' Rhina Delgado, (phone), and my license number G355268. ... So when I call, you know it's me. What time did we say again?"

5. Calendar. "Can you put it in your phone right now while we're on? ... I'll wait."

6. Digital card. If you haven't sent it yet, send it now while still on the line: "I just sent you my card. ... Did it come through?"

7. Grace and respect. "Sometimes these run a little long, so give me about 15 minutes of grace on either side, and I'll do the same for you. If anything changes, let me know right away — I don't want to miss helping another family with that time."

8. Tie back to their why. "Before I let you go — you told me (their reason from the burden question) is why you started looking. That's exactly what we're going to take care of on (day)."

"Have a great day, and we'll talk (day) at (time)!"

Do NOT say "thank you" here — it sounds like they did you a favor, not the other way around.

Then: text a confirmation the day before and one hour before.

13. Objections at the booking

Every objection comes down to fear of change. Don't argue and don't buy their story. Help them see what it's costing them to stay the same.

"It's too expensive" — comparative frame

concerned "I hear you. Everything costs more right now. ... But in reality, which costs more? ... Setting aside a little each month... and when something happens to you, the house is paid off and (spouse) and the kids stay home... or doing nothing... and (spouse) has six months before she has to sell the house? ... In reality... which is more expensive?"

Then the intention frame: "Because I'm sure your real intention isn't to keep that $(amount) a month in the bank and hope nothing happens. ... I'm sure your real intention is to know your family keeps the home. ... So what puts you in the best position to do that?"

"I need to talk to my spouse" — The Spouse Stall

  1. Disarm. "That's not a problem at all. Honestly, I'd want both of you there — this covers the house you both live in. What time tomorrow are you both home?" Book it. Stay on the line.
  2. Get their concerns. curious "What questions do you think (spouse) might have?" (now they tell you their own worries)
  3. Devil's advocate, with the outcome. concerned "My only concern for you is... what happens if (spouse) says 'let's look at it later'... and something happens before later comes? Who keeps the house for you and the kids?"
  4. Pain recap. "Earlier you told me (their pain). Do you want that fixed? ... Whose job is it to fix it? ... So what do you feel we should do?"
  5. Future pain. challenge "If another year goes by and nothing changes... and something happens... what happens to your family and the house?" Let them say it out loud.

Why both spouses: in mortgage protection the spouse is usually being covered too and must answer their own health questions. Booking both is the honest move — never coach anyone to go around their spouse.

"I need to think about it"

curious "Of course. ... Just so I understand... what part do you want to think about... the price... or whether (spouse) really needs the house paid off?" (they'll tell you the real objection)

"Can you just email me the options?"
"I can do better than that — we'll go over them together, so you can ask questions and I can make sure it fits your health and budget. Let me just confirm your information..."

"I already talked to an agent"
"That makes sense. Every agent is different — some only work with one or two companies. I work with a lot of carriers, so there's no harm in comparing. Did they show you more than one option, or just one price?"

"What's an IMO?"
"It's an agency that partners with a lot of insurance companies at once. Instead of being tied to one company, I can shop your case across all of them."

If they mention a notice or letter: "Go ahead and put that notice to the side — we'll take care of it from here."

If things are tight: "What I'm hearing is things are tight right now." Then offer something sized to that. If they mention a child, build the solution around protecting that child.

14. VA loan leads — modifiers

1. How to sound — read this first

Say WHEN, never IF. "When something happens to you..." There is no "if" with death. "If" lets their brain say "not me" and relax.

1. Introducción y cómo funciona

"Solo para que sepas cómo va a ir esta llamada, soy lo que llaman una suscriptora e intermediaria de campo. Me reúno con un par de docenas de familias por semana para determinar si la protección hipotecaria es una necesidad o un lujo. Legalmente no podemos sobre-asegurarte, y éticamente no queremos sub-asegurarte. Mi trabajo es construir un caso de idoneidad para que podamos aprobarte hoy."

"Te haré algunas preguntas básicas sobre tu salud, finanzas e hipoteca. Una vez que revisemos eso, normalmente se vuelve obvio si esto es algo que necesitas. Representamos a más de 30 compañías calificadas A — nombres que probablemente conoces, como Mutual of Omaha, Fidelity y Foresters."

Por qué las familias eligen la protección hipotecaria: las familias ya tienen bolsas de activos — acciones, 401(k)s, seguro a través del trabajo — y no quieren que esa bolsa se vaya a una compañía hipotecaria cuando fallezcan, o que se vea afectada por impuestos. Ahí es donde entra la protección hipotecaria.

2. Verificar hipoteca y presupuesto
  • Verifica el monto de la hipoteca, el pago mensual, si fue refinanciamiento o compra nueva, y cuánto tiempo planean quedarse
  • Ingreso total del hogar vs. pago mensual de la hipoteca (cálculo aproximado)
  • Anota el ingreso por persona y tipo de ingreso en la hoja — escribirlo juntos ayuda a construir el "porqué"
  • Pregunta sobre pagos de auto, seguro, suscripciones, guardería, teléfono, servicios, comida, dinero de gustos, deudas
  • ¿Tienen 401k, ahorros o seguro de vida ya vigente? Nombra cada activo y su propósito — ¿quieren usarlo para pagar la hipoteca? (No.)

"Entonces al final de cada mes les queda ____ — ¿es correcto, o mis números están un poco equivocados?" Logra que acuerden un monto; esto ayuda a superar el "necesito pensarlo" más adelante.

3. Verificar información de salud

Confirma estatura, peso, medicamentos, historial de salud, uso de nicotina/tabaco. "Todo lo que me digas es solo para ayudarte a colocarte con la cobertura correcta, para que podamos aprobarte hoy."

  • ¿Qué medicamentos te ha recetado el doctor?
  • ¿Cuándo fue la última vez que estuviste hospitalizado(a)?
  • ¿Alguna otra situación de salud en los últimos 15 años?

Nada es "grave," aunque lo sea — que se sientan cómodos compartiendo. Sé curiosa y asertiva.

4. Cerrar la brecha — preguntas de suposición

Menores de 50 (pago total/parcial): "Supongo que la razón por la que estamos viendo esto es porque quieres asegurarte de que tu cónyuge e hijos no tengan que perder la casa o mudarse con familiares si tú fallecieras o te enfermaras y no pudieras hacer los pagos — ¿o estoy entendiendo mal?"

60+, cónyuge es beneficiario: "Supongo que quieres asegurarte de que no perdamos la casa ni el patrimonio, o que no entremos en ejecución hipotecaria — ¿o estoy entendiendo mal?"

60+, hijos son beneficiarios: "Supongo que quieres asegurarte de que tus hijos no carguen con la hipoteca y terminen perdiendo la casa."

Entre 50 y 60 años: depende de la salud. Saludable → pago total/parcial. No saludable → protección del período crítico/patrimonio.

Profundizando: "Si mañana tu mejor amigo(a) ___ no llegara a casa, ¿cuál es el plan actual?" ¿Quién se vería afectado, cuánto tiempo podrían sobrevivir, tendrían que vender, podrían mantener la hipoteca con un solo ingreso?   2. Saca sus dudas. curiosa "¿Qué preguntas crees que (cónyuge) podría tener?" (ahora te dicen sus propias preocupaciones)
  3. Abogada del diablo, con el resultado. preocupada "Mi única preocupación por ti es... ¿qué pasa si (cónyuge) dice 'luego lo vemos'... y algo pasa antes de que llegue ese luego? ¿Quién se queda con la casa para ti y los niños?"
  4. Repasa el dolor. "Antes me dijiste (su dolor). ¿Quieres que eso se resuelva? ... ¿De quién es la responsabilidad de resolverlo? ... Entonces, ¿qué sientes que deberíamos hacer?"
  5. Dolor futuro. firme "Si pasa otro año y nada cambia... y algo pasa... ¿qué le pasa a tu familia y a la casa?" Deja que lo digan en voz alta.

Por qué los dos: en protección hipotecaria el cónyuge casi siempre también se asegura y tiene que contestar sus propias preguntas de salud. Agendar a los dos es lo honesto — nunca le enseñes a nadie a pasar por encima de su cónyuge.

Why our calls sound different

Tone and questions beat a memorized pitch

Leads hear the same words from every agent, so they put their guard up in the first few seconds. Two agents can read the same script and get completely different results — the difference is how it sounds and what gets asked next.

  • Say "when," never "if." Death is certain. "If" lets them relax.
  • Five tones: curious (to learn), confused (to get them to explain), concerned (for pain), challenge (only after the pain is built), playful (to drop their guard).
  • Slow down. The pause after a question is where the real answer comes.
  • Repeat their emotion word. "Worried..." — then stay quiet.
  • Find more than one problem. One problem is a small gap. Three problems is a reason to act.
  • Let them say it. What they tell themselves is far more convincing than anything you tell them.

Practice each tone out loud on a family member before you use it on a lead. It will feel scripted at first. That's normal.
