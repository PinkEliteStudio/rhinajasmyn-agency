7 · Knowing your options

The first type of plan is a full mortgage payoff. That wipes out the mortgage and leaves the house free and clear. It gets harder to qualify for as we get older or develop health issues, but that is one type.

The other type is called Mortgage Payment Protection. That's what most families in your situation go with, and based on what you've told me, it's what I recommend for you too. Three reasons:

One, it's the most affordable. Two, it's the easiest to qualify for. Three, it does the most important thing you want: your family is not stuck with the mortgage when you pass away.

Here's how it works. It does not always wipe out the mortgage. But picture this:

Your family is going through the funeral and the grief, and the mortgage company calls and says, "We're so sorry for your loss. Why don't you take the next year off and not make any payments while you get through this."

Pause. Smile.

That would be nice, wouldn't it? … But we know they don't do that. They want their payment. And we all saw in 2008 and 2009, if they don't get it, they have no problem taking the house.

That's exactly what this plan does. It covers the mortgage payments for a period of time you choose: a few months, a year, a year and a half. It buys your family time, so nobody is sitting at your funeral worried about a payment due in two weeks.

Why time matters (the equity story)

Let me tell you a true story that was shared in our company. A man named Brad lost both of his parents in a car accident. Their house was worth about $400,000 and they owed about $250,000. So there was $150,000 in equity they always meant for Brad and his sister.

What they didn't plan for was the kids inheriting the debt. Neither of them could make the payments. So they had equity, but no time.

While they were grieving, the mortgage company wanted its payment. Then one of those "We Buy Houses" investors called. You've seen those signs and letters, right? They always reach out after someone passes.

He offered $250,000. The house was worth $400,000. But they felt the pressure, so they sold. And $150,000 their parents worked for was gone.

If they had even six months or a year of payments covered, they could have grieved, cleaned up the house, listed it with a realtor, and sold it for what it was worth. Not having protection cost them $150,000.

8 · How it works

Level death benefit

The amount never goes down. If your plan covers 12 months of payments, your family gets 12 months whether it's next week or 20 years from now.

Benefit goes to your family

The check goes to your family, not the mortgage company. That gives them control to use the money however they need, when they need it. The mortgage, the funeral, fixing up the house to sell. Their choice.

Fixed premiums

Your price never goes up and the coverage doesn't run out halfway through your mortgage, when you're older and it's harder or more expensive to qualify.

Portable

It's tied to you, not the loan. Sell, move, or refinance, and your coverage goes with you.

Cash value

On plans that build cash value, you can borrow against it if the AC goes out, or if you ever don't need the coverage, you can surrender it and get some of your money back.

Only say this for the products you're quoting that actually build cash value.

Extra for an accident

The top three causes of death in the U.S. are heart disease, cancer, and accidents. Your plan pays the months of payments for any cause. If it's an accident, it pays an extra amount on top, often enough to pay off the house. I'll show you that amount on each option.

No medical exam

No nurses, no needles. The application takes about 10 to 15 minutes. They ask health questions and check your prescription history, and many times we get an answer right away.

If the carrier isn't instant decision, give the usual time frame (a few days to a week).

Any questions so far?
